# Noether-guided canonicalization — three curvature probes

**Status:** T0 executable research note; extends the dimensional resource
calibration without promoting a Theory Map or API claim.

## 1. Question and dimensional correction

Can symmetry provide a discovery mechanism for canonicalization and
complexity resources?  The motivating analogy needs one correction:

\[
\text{time translation}\leftrightarrow\text{energy},
\qquad
\text{space translation}\leftrightarrow\text{momentum},
\]

but computational time is not energy and computational space is not momentum.
Energy and momentum are covectors conjugate to time and space; dimensionally,

\[
[E][T]=[p][L]=[\text{action}].
\]

Likewise, complexity resources accumulate or aggregate along histories, while
Noether charges are dual data generated by symmetries of an action.  The
candidate discovery chain is therefore

\[
\text{process}
\to \text{symmetry}
\to \text{quotient/history lift}
\to \text{moment map and connection}
\to \text{task resource}
\to \text{optimization}.
\]

This note probes that chain in anisotropic optics, a magnetic connection, and
Berry phase.

## 2. Anisotropic optics — a positive Noether/Fermat case

Let each optical layer carry a positive one-homogeneous Finsler density

\[
F_i(v)=n_i\sqrt{\alpha_i^2v_x^2+v_y^2}.
\]

Translation along a flat interface is a continuous symmetry.  Its Noether
covector is the tangential optical momentum

\[
p_x=\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial v_x}
=\frac{n_i\alpha_i^2v_x}
{\sqrt{\alpha_i^2v_x^2+v_y^2}}.
\]

For endpoints ((0,4)) and ((6,-8)), the executable calibration uses

\[
(n_1,\alpha_1)=(1,1),
\qquad
(n_2,\alpha_2)=(1/2,2).
\]

At interface crossing (x=3), the two segment norms are five and ten, and

\[
p_x^{(1)}=\frac35
=\frac12\frac{4\cdot3}{10}=p_x^{(2)}.
\]

The optical-length function is strictly convex, so this Noether matching is
also the unique Fermat minimum.  This gives a successful discovery pattern:
interface symmetry selects the conserved task covector rather than requiring
the generalized Snell invariant to be guessed.

## 3. Magnetic connection — genuine curvature and holonomy

For a constant magnetic field (B), choose the local symmetric gauge

\[
A=\frac B2(-y\,dx+x\,dy),
\qquad dA=B\,dx\wedge dy.
\]

On a two-by-three rectangle with (B=5), the exact loop integral is

\[
\oint A=30.
\]

Under (A\mapsto A+d\chi), every open-path representative changes by
(\chi(q_1)-\chi(q_0)), but the closed holonomy remains 30.  More strongly,
two histories with the same endpoints differ by the enclosed flux in every
gauge.  Thus the quotient endpoint is insufficient: the lifted history and
connection retain observable information.

This is the first calibration here with nonzero curvature.  It also exposes a
limit of scalar Bellman language.  Reversing the loop changes the sign of the
magnetic action, so (\int A) is composable history data but not a positive cost
by itself.  Physical stationary action includes both kinetic and connection
terms; it must not be silently replaced by shortest path.

## 4. Berry phase — history payload before resource order

For a spin-half eigenline transported once around a cone,

\[
\frac{\gamma_B}{2\pi}=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\pmod 1.
\]

Taking (\cos\theta=3/5) gives (\gamma_B/2\pi=1/5).  A gauge change shifts the
representative by an integer turn, while the (U(1)) holonomy is unchanged.
Reversal gives the inverse (4/5), and their composition is zero modulo one.

Consequently phase cannot itself be a nonnegative additive complexity.  A
measurement task may map it to an interference scalar such as
(\cos\gamma_B), but that map is not additive along histories.  Canonical
history therefore carries a more general payload before a task extracts an
ordered resource.

## 5. Revised candidate structure

The resource bundle in the previous note is too narrow as the most primitive
object.  A safer candidate is

\[
\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathcal C,
\]

where (\mathcal K) is a bundle of composable history payloads with connection.
A task supplies

\[
\Phi_T:\mathcal K\to\mathcal R_T,
\qquad
\lambda_T:\mathcal R_T\to\overline{\mathbb R},
\]

where (\mathcal R_T) has the order and composition needed for optimization.
Only after these maps does an ordinary scalar Bellman value necessarily
appear.

The three examples separate three claims:

| Probe | What symmetry discovers | Optimization status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| anisotropic optics | conserved tangential optical momentum | positive convex Fermat/Bellman cost |
| magnetic connection | gauge class, curvature, flux holonomy | stationary signed action; not bare shortest path |
| Berry eigenline | (U(1)) phase holonomy | task observable exists, no intrinsic cost order |

## 6. Consequences for computational complexity

Time-like accumulated cost is often additive, but space complexity is commonly
a peak resource:

\[
S(\gamma_1\circ\gamma_2)
=\max(S(\gamma_1),S(\gamma_2)),
\]

not a momentum or an additive sum.  Memory-time volume
(\int M(t)dt) is additive, while peak memory uses a max composition law.
Therefore the task resource may be an ordered semiring or monoid rather than a
vector space.

The refined conjecture is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{complexity is a task evaluation of transported history payloads;
Noether charges identify symmetry-dual components but are not the cost itself.}
}
\]
