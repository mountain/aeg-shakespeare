# Oscillator II: coefficient extension before spectral semantics

**Status:** one narrow new discovery operation; no general spectral API.

## 1. Starting point

Oscillator I ended with a process-first statement:

\[
D x=p,\qquad Dp=-x
\]

generates a two-dimensional additive grammar and the shortest grammar-wide relation

\[
\boxed{D^2+1=0.}
\]

The relation was obtained before eigenvalues, complex exponentials, or Fourier language entered the presentation.

Over the current rational/real coefficient language, that process polynomial is irreducible.  The next question is therefore not yet "what are the eigenvalues?" but a smaller representation question:

> What happens if the coefficient language itself is explicitly enlarged?

## 2. An extension is a proposal, not an automatic theorem

The new operation is

```text
factor_process_relation_over_extension(relation, extension)
```

It takes an already-discovered process relation and a caller-declared algebraic extension accepted by the symbolic backend.

For the oscillator the caller proposes adjoining

\[
i^2=-1.
\]

Only under that proposal does

\[
D^2+1
\]

refine to

\[
\boxed{(D-i)(D+i).}
\]

The routine verifies that the returned primary factors multiply back to the original monic process relation.  It does not attach root or spectral semantics to them.

## 3. Reuse relation kernels rather than adding eigenvectors

The existing generic relation-kernel machinery already asks for primitives satisfying a process relation.  Applying it to the two new order-one factors gives one-dimensional kernels:

\[
(D-i)f=0,
\qquad
(D+i)g=0.
\]

The discovered primitives are, up to nonzero scalar multiples,

\[
f=x-ip,
\qquad
g=x+ip.
\]

They span the original additive grammar after the coefficient language has been enlarged.  Thus the complete executable chain is

\[
\boxed{
\text{process}
\to
\text{finite additive grammar}
\to
D^2+1
\to
\text{explicit coefficient extension}
\to
(D-i)(D+i)
\to
\text{one-dimensional relation kernels}.
}
\]

Only after this chain has been constructed do the familiar words "eigenmode" or "spectral coordinate" become classical interpretations.

## 4. What the example teaches about representation

There are now two valid presentations of the same process grammar.

In the original coefficient language:

\[
V=\operatorname{span}\{x,p\}
\]

is one irreducible two-dimensional relation component.

After adjoining \(i\):

\[
V_{\mathbb C}=V_+\oplus V_-
\]

splits into two one-dimensional relation components.

Neither presentation is free.  The second reduces component dimension but enlarges the coefficient language.  Shakespeare currently has no cost axis for that trade-off.

This is more informative than simply declaring the complex basis superior: it identifies the next missing tool precisely.

## 5. Five-line research ledger

**Primitive assumptions.** The harmonic-oscillator process, additive-span closure from Oscillator I, and the already-discovered relation `D^2+1`.

**Forbidden structures.** Predeclared eigenvalues/eigenvectors, action matrix as ontology, complex normal coordinate, trigonometric/Fourier solution, general spectral theorem.

**Discovered structure.** Under the explicit proposal to adjoin `i`, the order-two relation splits into two order-one factors whose exact kernels are generated by `x-ip` and `x+ip`.

**New reusable abstraction.** Factor an existing process relation after a caller-declared algebraic coefficient extension, with exact factor-product certification.

**Unresolved manual choice.** Shakespeare does not yet decide whether the gain from finer decomposition justifies the cost of extending the coefficient language.

## 6. Why we stop here

A tempting next move would be to introduce a large `Spectrum`, `EigenMode`, or harmonic-analysis hierarchy.  This vignette gives no reason to do so yet.

The immediate pressure is narrower: representation cost currently prices grammar, relations, history, decoding, and task error, but not the complexity of the **coefficient language itself**.  A future calibration should first decide whether coefficient extension deserves an explicit cost/certificate before any general spectral abstraction is promoted.

Only after that should the translation example ask how a whole commuting family of processes leads from repeated one-dimensional characters toward Fourier analysis.
