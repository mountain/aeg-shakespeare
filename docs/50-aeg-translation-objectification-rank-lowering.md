# AEG Translation Objectification and Compositional Rank Lowering

**Status:** research calibration of V1 -> V2 -> V3 -> V4 in `docs/THEORY_MAP.md`; no generic objectification or rank API is proposed.

## 0. Purpose

The structural audit in `49-theory-implementation-structural-alignment.md` found that the vertical Process Geometry program had good free-generation infrastructure but no executable example satisfying the full sequence

\[
\text{semantic compression}
\to
\text{objectification}
\to
\text{new free composition}
\to
\text{compositional rank lowering}.
\]

This note supplies the smallest arithmetic/AEG calibration in which all four steps can be stated and checked exactly.

The example is deliberately elementary. Its role is not mathematical novelty; it is to determine what an executable **rank transition** must retain before the repository attempts a general abstraction or moves upward to Multiplication and A/M analysis.

The lower-rank process is the signed unit-translation language generated by

\[
S:x\mapsto x+1,
\qquad
P:x\mapsto x-1.
\]

Literal histories are free words in \(S,P\). Their exact translation semantics is the net displacement. Stable semantic classes are then objectified into rank-one translation primitives

\[
T_n:x\mapsto x+n.
\]

Those new primitives may be freely composed. Every such higher-rank word is lowered back into the lower-rank language, and lowering is required to preserve composition **up to the declared lower-rank semantic equivalence**.

This is the first executable target in the repository where the word `objectification` is justified by the full `docs/44` gate rather than by naming a candidate primitive.

---

## 1. Rank 0 — free signed-successor histories

Let

\[
\Sigma_0=\{S,P\}
\]

with interpretations

\[
S(x)=x+1,
\qquad
P(x)=x-1.
\]

A literal lower-rank history is

\[
h\in\mathcal H_0=\{S,P\}^*.
\]

`ProcessWord` is already the correct executable carrier because it keeps order and provenance without assuming cancellation or commutativity.

Define the semantic displacement

\[
q(h)=\#_S(h)-\#_P(h)\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The action on an arbitrary integer state is then

\[
\llbracket h\rrbracket_0(x)=x+q(h).
\]

Two histories are semantically equivalent for the exact translation task iff

\[
h_1\sim h_2
\iff
q(h_1)=q(h_2).
\]

Thus, for example,

\[
SSPPSSP\sim S,
\]

when both have the same net displacement, despite having different literal histories.

---

## 2. V1 — semantic compression is a continuation congruence

The map

\[
q:\mathcal H_0\to\mathbb Z
\]

is not merely a terminal-state compression. It is compatible with every continuation:

\[
q(hk)=q(h)+q(k).
\]

Therefore

\[
h_1\sim h_2
\Longrightarrow
h_1k\sim h_2k
\qquad
\forall k\in\mathcal H_0.
\]

This is the essential V1 property. The semantic classes are reusable because continuation cannot expose information that the quotient discarded.

The quotient can be identified with

\[
\mathcal C_0\cong\mathbb Z,
\]

where each integer records one exact translation action.

This is a simple infinite analogue of the continuation-stability discipline established by the finite exact quotient in H1.

---

## 3. V2 — objectification

Now promote a stable semantic class into a new primitive:

\[
\operatorname{Obj}(n)=T_n.
\]

The meaning of \(T_n\) is not “the particular lower history used when it was discovered.” Its meaning is the entire semantic class

\[
[h]\quad\text{with}\quad q(h)=n.
\]

Thus objectification erases lower-rank construction accidents while retaining exact translation semantics.

This passes the first objectification gates:

1. **semantic stability** — \(n\) determines the exact translation task;
2. **continuation compatibility** — equality of \(n\) is a congruence;
3. **new primitive identity** — \(T_n\) is usable without replaying one chosen lower history.

But objectification would still be weak if \(T_n\) could only be named once. The next requirement is generativity.

---

## 4. V3 — free higher-rank composition

Let

\[
\Sigma_1=\{T_n:n\in\mathbb Z\}
\]

or, in a bounded discovery experiment, any selected finite subset of such primitives.

The higher-rank language is initially free:

\[
\mathcal H_1=\mathsf F(\Sigma_1).
\]

A word

\[
H=T_{n_1}T_{n_2}\cdots T_{n_k}
\]

has higher-rank semantics

\[
\llbracket H\rrbracket_1(x)
=x+\sum_i n_i.
\]

The important point is that after objectification the primitives may participate in composites that were never individually enumerated in the lower histories used to identify them.

For example, if a discovery phase retains only

\[
T_2,\quad T_{-1},
\]

then the free higher-rank word

\[
T_2T_2T_{-1}
\]

already denotes translation by \(+3\), without requiring \(+3\) to have been supplied as a primitive candidate.

That new compositional search space is what distinguishes objectification from memoization.

---

## 5. V4 — rank lowering

Choose a canonical lower-rank representative for one objectified primitive:

\[
L(T_n)=
\begin{cases}
S^n,&n>0,\\
\varepsilon,&n=0,\\
P^{-n},&n<0.
\end{cases}
\]

Extend lowering from generators to **all legal higher-rank words** by concatenation:

\[
L(T_{n_1}\cdots T_{n_k})
=
L(T_{n_1})\cdots L(T_{n_k}).
\]

The defining semantic condition is

\[
q(L(H))
=
\sum_i n_i.
\]

Equivalently, for every state \(x\),

\[
\boxed{
\llbracket L(H)\rrbracket_0(x)
=
\llbracket H\rrbracket_1(x).
}
\]

This is stronger than providing a decoder for isolated primitives. The interpretation is defined on every legal composite.

It also preserves higher-rank composition:

\[
q\bigl(L(H_1H_2)\bigr)
=
q\bigl(L(H_1)\bigr)
+
q\bigl(L(H_2)\bigr).
\]

Literal lower words need not be equal. The contract is equality under the declared lower semantic quotient.

This is exactly the distinction made in `docs/44`: rank lowering is generally semantics-preserving, not a syntactic inverse of objectification.

---

## 6. Relation soundness

Once the higher-rank language exists, one may propose relations such as

\[
T_mT_n\equiv T_{m+n}.
\]

Such a relation is legitimate only if its two sides lower to equivalent lower-rank semantics:

\[
q\bigl(L(T_mT_n)\bigr)
=m+n
=q\bigl(L(T_{m+n})\bigr).
\]

Likewise

\[
T_mT_n\equiv T_nT_m
\]

is sound for this translation process because both lower to net displacement \(m+n\).

The executable calibration therefore checks three distinct obligations:

1. **generator meaning** — every \(T_n\) lowers;
2. **composition soundness** — arbitrary words lower compositionally;
3. **relation soundness** — proposed high-rank equalities remain valid below.

These are the minimum V4 obligations that a later generic rank-lowering abstraction would have to expose.

---

## 7. Complexity advantage: only a local statement here

A canonical lower representation of \(T_n\) has \(|n|\) unit steps, while the objectified language uses one primitive carrying parameter \(n\).

This demonstrates a representation-depth reduction, but it should not be overstated as an asymptotic computational theorem. Encoding the integer parameter itself costs roughly logarithmic information, and a concrete execution backend may expand the operation again.

The valid claim is narrower:

> objectification can move repeated lower-rank process structure from history depth into primitive description.

Future work should compare this tradeoff with intrinsic distinguishability complexity rather than treating one high-rank symbol as cost-free.

## 8. Red team — terminal compression is not enough

A tempting weaker quotient records only absolute displacement:

\[
r(h)=|q(h)|.
\]

Then

\[
r(S)=r(P)=1,
\]

so a terminal observer might merge \(S\) and \(P\).

But this equivalence is not continuation-stable. Continue both histories by \(S\):

\[
r(SS)=2,
\qquad
r(PS)=0.
\]

The continuation exposes the discarded sign.

Therefore `abs(net displacement)` cannot support exact reusable objectification for the translation task. It may be valid for a weaker terminal-only task, but it cannot be silently promoted into a primitive whose meaning is expected to survive arbitrary future composition.

This red team demonstrates why V1 semantic compression must precede V2 objectification.

---

## 9. Why this is an AEG calibration

The lower process is the elementary Addition/translation direction from which AEG began. The construction does not yet use the full Addition/Multiplication frame, but it makes one arithmetic-tower idea executable:

```text
unit process history
  -> stable translation semantics
  -> translation object
  -> new free translation language
  -> compositional interpretation back to unit process
```

The next arithmetic rank should add a multiplicative/dilation object acting on translation objects. That step is more informative because the relation is no longer merely additive composition; it should expose how Multiplication reorganizes the Addition geometry.

Only after that explicit rank transition exists should the project test the stronger analytic-closure question using the existing A/M calculus:

\[
A=\partial_a,
\qquad
M=\partial_v+a\partial_a,
\qquad
[A,M]=A.
\]

Thus this calibration is deliberately the vertical foundation for a later A/M cross-rank analysis rather than a substitute for it.

## 10. API consequence

None yet.

The executable implementation should remain inside a research test. A single arithmetic example is not enough evidence for generic classes named `Objectification`, `ProcessRank`, `RankLowering`, or `SemanticClosure`.

What this example can do is constrain any future Experimental design. A candidate generic vertical API must at least represent:

- lower semantic equivalence;
- objectified generator meaning;
- higher-rank free composition;
- a lowering interpretation on every legal term;
- relation soundness;
- a red-team boundary where terminal compression fails continuation compatibility.

A design that cannot express all six has lost semantics already visible in the first calibration.

## 11. Theory Impact

**Theory position:** V1 semantic compression -> V2 objectification -> V3 higher-rank free composition -> V4 compositional rank lowering.

**Maturity:** concrete research calibration in the Addition/translation model organism; no generic Experimental abstraction yet.

**Semantic claim:** signed unit-translation histories admit an exact continuation congruence by net displacement; its semantic classes can be objectified into translation primitives whose arbitrary free composites admit composition-preserving lowering to the original history semantics.

**Non-claim:** this does not establish a universal objectification mechanism, a general process-rank category, Multiplication rank raising, Arithmetic Universality, topology, or analytic closure.

**Evidence:** exact integer semantics, explicit free-word composition, exact lowering identity on arbitrary generated test words, relation-soundness checks, and the absolute-displacement non-congruence red team.

**Map effect:** supplies the first complete concrete V1 -> V4 vertical calibration while leaving V5 and generic V2–V4 abstractions at research status.

**Migration risk:** low because all implementation remains research-local; the calibration constrains future abstractions without creating compatibility pressure.
