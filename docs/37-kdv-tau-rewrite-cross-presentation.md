# KdV tau combinatorics and history rewriting as a commuting square

**Status:** Level-2 cross-presentation calibration; executable, not a public API contract.

## 1. Why a second representation matters

The preceding KdV rewrite experiment established a nontrivial statement inside one
presentation: the classical pair phase law gives a parameterized local history
relation whose three-soliton critical pair joins, while a state-dependent impostor
can preserve every local phase-sum check and still fail confluence.

That was evidence that

```text
local pair law + global joinability
```

contains more information than two-body conservation alone.  But the interaction
factor itself was still supplied from classical KdV soliton theory.

The next threshold is therefore cross-presentation:

> can a second representation, starting from different primitive syntax, force the
> same pair data and the same three-body composition law?

For this calibration the second representation is Hirota's bilinear tau language.

## 2. Bilinear KdV as the independent starting point

Use the standard KdV normalization

\[
u_t+6uu_x+u_{xxx}=0,
\]

with the Hirota bilinear equation

\[
\boxed{(D_xD_t+D_x^4)\tau\cdot\tau=0}.
\]

For an exponential support subset \(S\), define

\[
K_S=\sum_{i\in S}k_i,
\qquad
\Omega_S=-\sum_{i\in S}k_i^3.
\]

The bilinear operator acts on two exponential terms by the exact algebraic kernel

\[
\mathcal H(S,T)
=(K_S-K_T)(\Omega_S-\Omega_T)+(K_S-K_T)^4.
\]

This means the first two nontrivial soliton coefficients can be solved without
performing any differential calculus on explicit exponentials.  The executable
vignette `tests/research/test_kdv_tau_rewrite_cross_presentation.py` works entirely
with finite support labels, symbolic coefficients, and this kernel.

## 3. The two-body factor is derived rather than inserted

Start with

\[
\tau_2
=1+z_1+z_2+A_{12}z_1z_2,
\]

where the exponentials \(z_i\) have dispersion \(\Omega_i=-k_i^3\), but
\(A_{12}\) is unknown.

Collecting the \(z_1z_2\) sector of the bilinear equation gives one linear equation
for \(A_{12}\).  Solving it produces

\[
\boxed{
A_{12}
=\left(\frac{k_1-k_2}{k_1+k_2}\right)^2.
}
\]

This is exactly the interaction factor used by the independent history-rewrite
calibration.

The important direction of explanation is now

```text
bilinear KdV semantics
    -> pair factor

history scattering semantics
    -> pair factor
```

rather than one implementation calling the other.

## 4. The three-body coefficient is forced to factor

Write the three-soliton support as

\[
\tau_3=
1+z_1+z_2+z_3
+A_{12}z_1z_2+A_{13}z_1z_3+A_{23}z_2z_3
+C_{123}z_1z_2z_3.
\]

The three pair coefficients are first obtained from their own two-body sectors.
Only then is the occupancy-\((1,1,1)\) bilinear sector solved for the still unknown
\(C_{123}\).  The exact result is

\[
\boxed{
C_{123}=A_{12}A_{13}A_{23}.
}
\]

Thus pair factorization is not preloaded into the three-body tau coefficient in
this calibration.  It is forced by the same bilinear equation that fixed the pair
coefficients.

For an exact integer choice of three distinct wave numbers, the test also checks
every exponential-support sector generated by \(\tau_3\cdot\tau_3\), not only the
symbolically selected pair and triple sectors.  All residuals vanish.

## 5. The commuting square

Now compare the tau presentation with the earlier history presentation.

Start from the descending visible history

\[
3\;2\;1.
\]

Either braid normalization path

\[
s_1s_2s_1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
s_2s_1s_2
\]

contains exactly three adjacent crossings.  The history trace therefore carries
the multiplicative residual

\[
A_{32}A_{31}A_{21}.
\]

The tau calculation independently gives the coefficient of the full three-soliton
support.  The executable comparison is

\[
\boxed{
C_{123}^{\mathrm{tau}}
=
\prod_{\text{rewrite crossings}}A_{ij}
}
\]

for both rewrite histories.

The first cross-presentation square is therefore

```text
Hirota bilinear equation
        |                         |
        v                         v
  pair coefficients --------> C_123
        |                         ^
        |                         |
        +--> history rewrites ----+
             product of residuals
```

No physical-field decoder is needed for this comparison.

## 6. A stronger red team: confluence can survive while the square fails

The previous red team modified pair transport and therefore destroyed three-body
rewrite confluence.  The present red team attacks a different logical layer.

Keep all one- and two-body tau coefficients unchanged, but set

\[
\widetilde C_{123}
=\gamma A_{12}A_{13}A_{23},
\qquad \gamma\neq1.
\]

Nothing in the pairwise history presentation changes.  In particular:

- every pair interaction factor is unchanged;
- both braid histories still normalize to the same visible order;
- their accumulated pair factor is still
  \(A_{12}A_{13}A_{23}\);
- the previous phase-transport confluence remains intact.

Yet two failures appear immediately.

First, the three-body Hirota sector is nonzero.  The fake tau polynomial is not a
KdV three-soliton tau solution.

Second,

\[
\frac{\widetilde C_{123}}
     {\prod A_{ij}}-1
=\gamma-1\neq0.
\]

So pairwise rewriting is no longer a sufficient presentation of the fake tau
data.  An additional irreducible three-body generator would be required.

This yields a sharper logical hierarchy:

```text
local pair conservation
    < pairwise rewrite confluence
    < cross-presentation completeness.
```

Confluence is therefore useful evidence for integrability, but this experiment
shows why it should not be promoted into a universal definition.

## 7. New API pressure: the relation between presentations

The previous experiment created pressure for two possible objects:

- a parametric history relation;
- a confluence certificate.

The tau cross-calibration exposes a deeper missing object.  We now have two
representations of the same process sector with a concrete square that can either
commute or fail.

The natural abstraction is not yet a `KdVTauFunction`.  It is closer to

\[
\boxed{\Pi_1\longrightarrow\Pi_2}
\]

where the map records which task-relevant data survive or are reconstructed across
presentations and carries residual certificates for commutation.

Possible later names include `PresentationMorphism` or
`CrossPresentationCertificate`, but neither should be made public from one KdV
example.  The next independent place to look for the same structure is the
regularization line:

```text
physical Kepler presentation
        -> Sundman / Levi-Civita presentation
```

If the same notion of task-preserving reconstruction is forced there, a generic
presentation-morphism API would have substantially stronger justification.

## 8. What this changes in the integrability program

The working dominance target can now be stated more precisely.

An integrable process should not merely admit a terminating or confluent local
rewrite system.  We want a compact family of presentations whose residual data are
mutually reconstructible:

\[
\text{local PDE / process}
\leftrightarrow
\text{tau / algebraic presentation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{history rewrite presentation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{later Abelian presentation}.
\]

For KdV at the current level, the first nontrivial invariant across two such
presentations is the pair interaction factor and its three-body product.

A nearby nonintegrable or deliberately corrupted system may fail in different
ways:

- no low-degree invariant closes;
- local rewrites fail confluence;
- irreducible higher-body coefficients appear;
- one presentation requires extra generators to reconstruct another;
- presentation cost grows under the same budget.

This suggests that the eventual signal is not a single scalar
`IntegrabilityScore`, but a pattern of closure and reconstruction failures across a
network of presentations.

## 9. Immediate next threshold

The next KdV step should now move upward rather than adding more soliton count.
There are two high-value options:

1. derive the finite tau support itself from a bounded construction grammar rather
   than supplying the Boolean subset support; or
2. begin the finite-gap line and construct the first explicit map
   `spectral curve -> Abelian flow`, then compare its degeneration with the tau /
   rewrite sector already calibrated.

The second option has greater architectural value because it forces the currently
missing Abelian process-flow layer and begins the promised

\[
\text{finite gap}\to\text{pinched cycle}\to\text{soliton}
\]

bridge.

## 10. Claim boundary

The current result does not prove an arbitrary-\(N\) factorization theorem, derive
the Hirota bilinearization automatically, reconstruct \(u(x,t)\) from \(\tau\), or
establish a general presentation category.

Its narrower contribution is exact and useful:

> **the KdV bilinear presentation independently forces the same two-body interaction
> factor used by the history presentation, forces the three-body tau coefficient to
> be their product, and agrees exactly with the multiplicative residual accumulated
> along either three-soliton rewrite history; an irreducible three-body tau defect can
> preserve pairwise confluence while breaking both the bilinear equation and the
> cross-presentation reconstruction.**
