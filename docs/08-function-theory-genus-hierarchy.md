# Process Quotients and a First Function-Theory Genus Hierarchy

**Status:** calibration evidence, not a universality theorem.

The pendulum test shows that a constrained mechanical process can force a generic
genus-one quotient without angle/trigonometric language.  The next question is
whether genus is only an accident of that problem or a reusable structural
observable across classical systems.

## Even-power oscillators

Consider the dimensionless family

\[
\mathscr D x=p,
\qquad
\mathscr D p=-x^{m-1},
\]

for even `m`, with invariant

\[
H=\frac12p^2+\frac1m x^m.
\]

On an energy leaf `H=E`,

\[
\boxed{p^2=2E-\frac{2}{m}x^m}.
\]

This is already a hyperelliptic process quotient.  For generic nondegenerate
parameters, the smooth projective genus is

\[
g=\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

The first three even cases therefore give:

| potential degree | quotient degree | generic genus |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 2 | 0 |
| 4 | 4 | 1 |
| 6 | 6 | 2 |

No trigonometric, elliptic, or hyperelliptic function name is supplied to the
test.  The hierarchy is read from the algebraic quotient forced by the process
and invariant leaf.

## Cross-problem genus one

The canonical pendulum quotient has the form

\[
Y^2=2(E-U)(1-U^2),
\]

which is cubic in `U`.  The quartic oscillator quotient is

\[
p^2=2E-\frac12x^4.
\]

The equations and degeneration loci are different, yet both generic smooth
quotients have genus one.

This is the first small cross-problem calibration of the idea

\[
\text{different primitive processes}
\longrightarrow
\text{different reductions}
\longrightarrow
\text{same standard quotient class}.
\]

The result should not yet be called a universal normal form.  Genus alone does
not determine an elliptic curve, its periods, real dynamical regime, decoder,
or total presentation cost.  It is nevertheless a representation invariant
that can be discovered without naming the classical special function in
advance.

## Function-theory interpretation

The current evidence suggests a program rather than a conclusion:

\[
\text{process}
\to
\text{invariants/constraints}
\to
\text{task-sufficient quotient}
\to
\text{algebraic geometry}
\to
\text{candidate function theory}.
\]

A/M function theory is generated directly by Addition/Multiplication process
relations and their resonances.  The genus-one examples arise by a different
route: invariant reduction produces a curve whose natural process differential
is Abelian.  Higher even-power potentials already produce genus two.

The useful Shakespeare question is therefore not "which named special function
solves this ODE?" but:

> which low-complexity process/function language is forced or economical after
> the relevant history, constraint, invariant, and task quotients are taken?

Future tests should add Euler top and other classical integrable systems, then
compare not just genus but relation complexity, decoder complexity, history
lifts, periods/holonomy, and presentation cost.
