# Citation scope

Rigorous references in Shakespeare serve three distinct roles.

1. **Historical provenance** — who introduced or developed the classical construction, and in what context?
2. **Classical theorem support** — where can a reader verify the standard mathematical fact used in the classical shadow?
3. **Backend methodology** — where can a reader verify the algebraic/symbolic method used by the implementation (for example Gröbner-basis ideal reduction)?

A single citation should not be stretched across all three roles unless it genuinely supports them.

The process-first interpretation is the repository's own research interpretation.  It should not be made to look classical by attaching a historical citation immediately after it.  Preferred wording is explicit:

> **Shakespeare interpretation.** The period lattice may be read as a residual of lifted history after state return.

followed separately by citations for the classical facts about periods, covering spaces, or elliptic functions.

When a test derives a formula symbolically, cite the classical identification rather than importing the formula from the citation.  The executable derivation should remain auditable on its own.
