# Sonnet — Boltzmann H theorem, BBGKY, and rank-relative observers

Status: T0 initialization. This study has not yet produced a new H theorem, a microscopic-to-kinetic derivation, or a generic API.

## 1. Classical problem and sign convention

For a one-particle density \(f(t,x,v)\), Boltzmann’s functional is

\[
H[f]=\int f\log f\,dx\,dv,
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}H[f_t]=-D[f_t]\le 0,
\]

so thermodynamic entropy is conventionally \(S=-k_BH\), up to the usual additive and reference-measure choices.

This statement already mixes several levels that the study must keep typed:

1. an \(N\)-particle reversible Hamiltonian or hard-sphere history;
2. the exact marginal family \(f_N^{(s)}\) and the unclosed BBGKY hierarchy;
3. an incoming-collision closure or propagation-of-chaos statement;
4. a singular Boltzmann–Grad limit and its one-sided time orientation;
5. the resulting kinetic semigroup generated by the Boltzmann equation;
6. a monotone functional on that reduced state;
7. the correlations and history information discarded by the reduction.

The Liouville dynamics preserves fine-grained Gibbs entropy. The one-particle \(H\) theorem is therefore not a scalar monotonicity theorem on the exact microscopic state. It is a theorem on a reduced kinetic description after specific limiting and closure operations.

## 2. Responsible target

The weak target is a faithful A/M-native reexpression of the classical proof. The stronger discovery target is to decide whether the process structure itself selects the logarithmic covector and relative-entropy functional, rather than merely accepting them as supplied notation.

A successful account must identify:

- the literal reversible collision histories;
- the task that declares which observables count as state;
- the lift or collision-tree payload;
- the stopping rule, hierarchy truncation, or limiting closure;
- the quotient and decoder that produce a kinetic state;
- the semigroup on that state;
- the residual that detects missing reverse information.

Merely renaming \(f\log f\), the collision operator, or molecular chaos does not pass.

| Kinetic object | Process-geometry role | Required caution |

| Hard-sphere or Hamiltonian trajectory | literal history | reversible before reduction |
| \(f_N^{(s)}\) | order-\(s\) observer of the microscopic law | observer order is not automatically arithmetic rank |
| BBGKY hierarchy | exact but unclosed observer tower | the \(s\)th equation depends on \(s+1\) data |
| two-particle cumulant or incoming trace | candidate continuation residual | hard-core exclusion and the reference measure must be explicit |
| molecular chaos | oriented closure, section, or limiting factorization claim | not an exact quotient identity |
| collision tree or cumulant history | lifted payload | composition order and recollision data matter |
| Boltzmann–Grad limit | singular, one-sided limiting morphism | irreversibility cannot be attributed to marginalization alone |

For hard spheres, the admissible \(N\)-particle measure is not a naive tensor product because of exclusion. Any entropy or mutual-information comparison must declare its reference measure and normalization.

## 4. Two independent axes: arithmetic rank and observer order

This Sonnet uses two indices that must never be collapsed:

- \(r\): process or arithmetic rank — the level at which prior processes have been objectified and freely composed;
- \(s\): observer or correlation order — how many particles, components, interfaces, or continuation slots are jointly inspected.

At each rank \(r\), the study may have a state space \(X_r\), a history space \(\mathcal H_r\), a task quotient \(Q_r\), an observer algebra \(\mathrm{Obs}_{r,Q}\), and a family \(f_r^{(s)}\). Moving horizontally in \(s\) refines observation and exposes correlations. Moving vertically in \(r\) changes the ontology of the objects being composed.

Thus increasing BBGKY order does not by itself raise arithmetic rank, and raising arithmetic rank is not the same as taking a higher particle marginal.

### 4.1 Observation at every rank

Suppose objectification supplies a lowering map

\[
L_r:X_{r+1}\longrightarrow \mathrm{Sem}(X_r).
\]

Every observer \(O\) on the lower-rank process semantics induces a higher-rank observer by pullback,

\[
L_r^*O = O\circ L_r.
\]

This gives a guaranteed class of lowering-induced observers at rank \(r+1\). It does not exhaust the possible observations there. Intrinsic rank-\((r+1)\) observers may inspect assembly brackets, object type, interaction graph, scale, reference measure, holonomy, or continuation residual that the lowering forgets.

The variance rule is fixed:

- states and executions move covariantly under lowering;
- predicates and measurements pull back contravariantly;
- an observer that does not factor through the declared quotient is a discriminator or residual;
- nonfactorization alone does not prove that a new state coordinate or a new rank has been earned.

### 4.2 A rank-relative H question

At a declared rank \(r\), task \(Q_r\), reference measure \(\mu_r\), collision or composition law, and closure may produce a semigroup \(B_{r,Q}\). The legitimate question is then whether there is a functional

\[
H_{r,Q,\mu_r}:X_{r,Q}\to\mathbb R
\]

with \(H_{r,Q,\mu_r}(B_{r,Q}(t)x)\le H_{r,Q,\mu_r}(x)\).

The study will not assume one scalar entropy valid at all ranks. It will ask:

1. which reference measure or detailed-balance structure selects the covector;
2. whether \(H_{r+1}\) lowers to, bounds, or decomposes relative to \(H_r\);
3. which observer families commute with lowering;
4. which residual measures the failure of such commutation;
5. when same-scale aggregation merely flattens by log-sum-exp and therefore does not raise rank.

## 5. A/M/P pressure point in the classical proof

For a binary collision, write

\[
X=\log(ff_*), \qquad Y=\log(f'f_*').
\]

The local entropy-production term becomes

\[
(e^Y-e^X)(Y-X)\ge 0.
\]

This exposes a specific A/M bridge:

- Multiplication forms the pair weights \(ff_*\) and \(f'f_*'\);
- the logarithmic chart sends products to Addition;
- the exponential chart returns to positive weights;
- collision reversal or detailed balance pairs the two orientations;
- monotonicity of the exponential produces a nonnegative flux–affinity pairing.

Likewise,

\[
\frac{\delta H}{\delta f}=1+\log f
\]

shows that the logarithm is the entropy covector, not merely a cosmetic coordinate change.

Phase 1 will treat this identity as a supplied baseline. A separate hidden-oracle track must test whether an A/M-native search can rediscover the same covector and functional without being handed \(H\), \(\log\), or the Maxwellian answer. Power has not been earned merely because the exponential appears.

### 5.1 Shannon entropy and the partition-function control

For a normalized finite law \(p\), Shannon entropy is

\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i.
\]

For an unnormalized density, a continuous density, or a hard-core gas, the measure and normalization are part of the object. The safer comparison is relative entropy with respect to a declared reference law or measure. The study will therefore not identify every residual count or observer multiplicity with Shannon entropy.

The equilibrium partition function

\[
Z(\beta)=\int e^{-\beta E(x)}\,d\mu(x)
\]

has a different role: it normalizes an exponential family, while \(\log Z\) is its additive chart and cumulant generator. The Gibbs variational principle links \(\log Z\), expected energy, and relative entropy, but this equilibrium variational identity is not the kinetic H theorem.

Partition functions nevertheless provide a sharp rank test. For independent assemblies with coherent measures,

\[
Z_{A\times B}=Z_AZ_B,
\qquad
\log Z_{A\times B}=\log Z_A+\log Z_B.
\]

That is same-scale multiplicative composition followed by an additive chart; it may flatten rather than raise arithmetic rank. A genuinely higher-rank partition object must therefore exhibit additional typed structure such as interaction, assembly brackets, a new reference measure, holonomy, or a nonflattening continuation residual.

## 6. Modern collision-history calibration

The long-time Boltzmann–Grad derivation of Deng, Hani, and Ma is an external calibration target, not an achieved equivalence. Its full collision histories, cumulant ansatz, molecules, cutting arguments, and norm estimates pressure-test the repository’s history, residual, quotient, and certificate vocabulary.

| Modern proof object | Candidate repository reading | What must still be proved |

## 7. Core research questions

1. Can the finite detailed-balance H theorem be expressed using only reversible collision histories, A/M-native coordinates, and a task-local quotient?
2. Can the logarithmic covector be discovered from a frozen candidate grammar rather than supplied?
3. What is the smallest exact continuation observable beyond the one-particle state that determines its next derivative?
4. Can that observable be related to BBGKY marginals, cumulants, or incoming collision traces without collapsing their distinctions?
5. Which one-sided limit or closure turns the reversible microscopic group into the kinetic semigroup?
6. Can time reversal be used to construct an explicit missing decoder?
7. At each arithmetic rank, which observers are lowering-induced and which are intrinsic?
8. Does a useful H-like Lyapunov functional exist on more than one rank, and if so how does it transform across the \((r,s)\) grid?

## 8. Claim boundaries and kill conditions

This Sonnet must be revised or stopped if it:

- calls the BBGKY index an arithmetic rank without a free-composition and lowering witness;
- attributes irreversibility to coarse-graining alone;
- treats molecular chaos as an exact equality at finite \(N\);
- calls a finite discriminator automatically stored state;
- equates correlation residuals with Shannon entropy without a declared measure;
- mixes continuous coarea, discrete shell count, memory multiplicity, and correlation entropy;
- invokes the H theorem with the wrong sign convention;
- uses a same-scale logarithmic aggregate as evidence of rank raising;
- claims a new proof while reproducing a supplied classical identity.
