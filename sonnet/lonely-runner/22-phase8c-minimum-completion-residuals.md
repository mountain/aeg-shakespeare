## 2. Primitive completion grammar

For each of the six branching parents, Phase 8C receives only its locally generated center-3 children.  Across all eight affected parents the local algorithm already generates 298 children rather than enumerating the full 72,241-state center-3 geometry.

For one completion parent, every center-3 wall-sign coordinate that varies among its possible children is an admissible primitive residual.  No preferred runner pair, target ratio, known five-wall global answer, or residual dimension is supplied.

Every candidate coordinate has the form

\[
\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac{u_j}{u_i}-c\right)
\in\{-1,0,+1\},
\]

where `c` is generated by equality of A/M contact times.

## 3. Exact minimum-cardinality search

For every pair of local children having different first-witness semantics, require the selected residual signature to distinguish them.

A candidate wall coordinate therefore covers exactly the cross-task child pairs on which its sign differs.  Finding a minimum set of wall coordinates becomes a finite exact set-cover problem.  Phase 8C solves it by dynamic programming over the uncovered conflict-pair bitset and then checks irredundancy by deleting each selected coordinate in turn.

This is a bounded exact optimization; the classical set-cover complexity background is standard [Karp-1972].  The interpretation of selected walls as process-completion residuals is Shakespeare/AEG-specific.

## 6. What "minimum completion" means here

Phase 8C proves minimum **raw wall cardinality** in a declared primitive grammar.  It does not yet prove minimum representation complexity.

For the four exact parents, the wall-sign signature itself is already a strong candidate new local primitive.

For the two over-refined parents, using the raw sign tuple as the final object would preserve distinctions that the task does not need.  These cases therefore force an additional step:

\[
\boxed{
\text{minimum raw wall support}
\to
\text{task-relative residual quotient/objectification}.
}
\]

This is closely aligned with Shakespeare's broader methodology: first identify the process directions needed for sufficiency; then quotient representation accidents rather than calling the first sufficient syntax canonical.
